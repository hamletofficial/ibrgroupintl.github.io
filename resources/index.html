What to consider when assessing a property

Evidence and certification

Check the scope, validity, date and issuing body of any certificate or performance claim. Distinguish a design target from verified, as-built performance.

Operational performance

Consider energy use, building services, controls, maintenance and how the space is occupied. Actual outcomes depend on both the building and its operation.

Occupier requirements

Connect building performance to comfort, ventilation, resilience, running costs and the needs of the intended occupier rather than relying on a single headline metric.

Using these resources in commercial leasing

When preparing or reviewing a listing, be specific about what is evidenced. State the certification or rating, its status and scope where verified; identify information that remains to be confirmed; and avoid implying that a building meets a standard solely because it includes selected design features.

Hamlet is intended to help property professionals keep sustainability information connected to the wider leasing conversation. The guides are educational material, not legal, engineering, energy-audit or certification advice.
